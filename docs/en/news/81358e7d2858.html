Secretary Mullin Observes Deportation Flight Removing Dangerous Illegal Aliens to El Salvador
Release Date: October 5, 2026
Among those deported are murderers, sexual assailants, and drunk drivers
WASHINGTON – On Friday, United States Department of Homeland Security (DHS) Secretary Markwayne Mullin observed a deportation flight out of Alexandria, Louisiana that removed 100 illegal aliens back to El Salvador, including those convicted of such crimes as murder, sexual assault, and driving while intoxicated.
The deportation flight out of Alexandria, Louisiana, bound for El Salvador
Every illegal alien on the flight was from El Salvador.
“The mission continues, with mass deportations underway,” said Secretary Mullin.“This deportation flight back to El Salvador included some of the worst illegal aliens, including convicted murderers, sexual assailants, and drunk drivers. DHS is delivering on President Trump’s mandate from the American people to carry out mass deportations so that we can make America safe again. We will never apologize for deporting illegal aliens, because mass deportations save lives.”
Among the illegal aliens deported on the most recent flight are:
Carlos Ovidio Culgua-Alas, whose criminal history includes convictions for homicide and alien in possession of a firearm, as well as arrests for public intoxication and disorderly conduct in Texas.
Jose Balmory Ramirez Rodriguez, an MS-13 gang member whose criminal history includes convictions for first-degree manslaughter with intent to cause serious physical injury and third-degree assault in New York.
Jose Martinez Flores, whose criminal history includes convictions for indecency with child – sexual contact and TWO counts of driving while intoxicated in Texas.
Jose Angel Ramos-Gutierrez, whose criminal history includes convictions for driving under the influence and gross child neglect creating risk of serious injury or death in West Virginia and an arrest for possession of cocaine in Maryland.
Paulino Barahona, whose criminal history includes a conviction for aggravated assault on a police officer and an arrest for driving under the influence in West Virginia.

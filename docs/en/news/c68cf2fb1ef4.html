ICE Reports Columbus Day Weekend Arrests of Noncitizens Convicted of Violent and Drug Crimes

Published Oct 13, 2026

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) announced that over the Columbus Day weekend it arrested at least 19 noncitizens who had prior criminal convictions, including murder, child sex offenses, rape, drug distribution and driving under the influence. The agency published the name, country of origin and underlying conviction for each individual, saying it will continue such arrests even where some jurisdictions decline to honor ICE detainers.

Key points

  • ICE reported arrests of at least 19 noncitizens with prior criminal convictions over the Columbus Day weekend.
  • Cited convictions include murder, homicide, vehicular homicide, child sexual abuse, rape, drug trafficking, firearms possession and DUI.
  • The individuals named came from Mexico, Guatemala, Ecuador, Colombia, Cuba and the Dominican Republic.
  • DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin said ICE will continue these arrests despite some jurisdictions not honoring ICE detainers.
  • The release describes individual enforcement actions and does not announce any new rule, fee or eligibility change.

Who is affected

Noncitizens with prior criminal convictions who are subject to ICE enforcement, and the communities where the arrests occurred.

USCIS Helps Florida Charge 16 Noncitizens With Unlawful Voting

Published Sep 29, 2026

USCIS assisted a Florida investigation that led to charges against 16 noncitizens accused of registering to vote by falsely claiming U.S. citizenship and then voting. Four additional individuals were charged with voting while on felony probation. USCIS confirmed the alien status of the individuals involved, according to the Florida Attorney General's Office.

Key points

  • Investigations began after immigration status reviews, including federal database checks, flagged registered voters who were not U.S. citizens.
  • USCIS played a key role in confirming the alien status of the individuals charged.
  • Most defendants are lawful permanent residents who falsely affirmed U.S. citizenship when registering and then voted in the 2024 elections.
  • Several defendants later signed a Citizenship Acknowledgement Form admitting they are not U.S. citizens.
  • Each charge is a third-degree felony, with most defendants facing up to 10 years in prison and one facing up to 20 years.
  • The cases span nine Florida counties and the investigation remains active, with more charges expected.

Who is affected

Noncitizens, including lawful permanent residents, who registered to vote or voted in Florida while not holding U.S. citizenship.

What to do

Noncitizens should be aware that registering or voting while not a U.S. citizen can lead to criminal charges and immigration consequences, and should consult an attorney regarding their status if they have concerns.

Release Date
09/29/2026
TALLAHASSEE, Fla. — U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services provided critical assistance to investigations that resulted in charges against 16 aliens accused of unlawfully voting in Florida elections. The Florida Office of the Attorney General made the announcement.
“U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services is leveraging its expertise to aid local investigations of aliens residing in the United States,” said USCIS Spokesman Zach Kahler. “Every day, the dedicated men and women of USCIS are working to root out immigration fraud wherever it occurs, hold bad actors accountable, and help protect the rights of American citizens.”
The investigations began with referrals from the Florida Department of State Office of Election Crimes and county Supervisors of Elections after immigration status reviews, including checks of federal databases, flagged registered voters who were not U.S. citizens. Florida Department of Law Enforcement investigators obtained voter registration applications, poll book signatures or vote-by-mail certificates, and post-election ineligibility notices to verify the crime had been committed. USCIS was instrumental in confirming individuals’ alien status.
The Office of Statewide Prosecution, working with the Florida Department of Law Enforcement, the Florida Department of State Office of Election Crimes, and USCIS has charged 16 aliens with registering to vote by falsely claiming U.S. citizenship and then voting. The office also charged four individuals with felony convictions who allegedly registered and voted while they remained on probation.
At the time of the announcement, Florida law enforcement had arrested multiple defendants, including:
Astrid Shamatu Navarrete Quintana, 24, of Fort Myers, a Mexican alien and lawful permanent resident since March 31, 2023. She registered on Aug. 19, 2024, falsely affirming she was a U.S. citizen, and voted in person on Nov. 5, 2024. She later signed a Citizenship Acknowledgement Form admitting she is not a U.S. citizen. She is charged with Unqualified Noncitizen Electors Willfully Voting and False Swearing.
Selvin Uton Whilby, 74, of Tampa, a Jamaican alien and lawful permanent resident since March 1, 1989. He registered in 2008, falsely affirming citizenship, and voted by mail in the 2024 primary and 2024 general elections as part of a vote-by-mail history dating to 2008. He later signed a form agreeing he is not a U.S. citizen. He is charged with two counts of Unqualified Noncitizen Electors Willfully Voting.
Cyntia Vanessa Canales, 62, of Ocala, a Chilean alien and lawful permanent resident. She registered in Marion County in 2012 and again in 2022, each time falsely affirming she was a U.S. citizen and listing her place of birth as Florida. She voted early in person on Oct. 27, 2024. She is charged with Unqualified Noncitizen Electors Willfully Voting.
Craig William Smith, 69, of Ocala, a Bahamian alien and lawful permanent resident since March 5, 1978. He registered on March 21, 2002, falsely affirming he was a U.S. citizen, and voted in the 2024 primary and 2024 general elections as part of a voting history dating to 2006. He later signed a Citizenship Acknowledgement Form admitting he is not a U.S. citizen. He is charged with two counts of Unqualified Noncitizen Electors Willfully Voting.
Tracey Sabrina Jones, 49, of Lakeland, a United Kingdom alien and lawful permanent resident since April 25, 1984. She registered in 1995, falsely affirming citizenship, and voted early in person on Oct. 26, 2024. She later signed a form admitting she is ineligible to register to vote. She is charged with Unqualified Noncitizen Electors Willfully Voting.
Kadian Gabrielle Amai Johnson, 29, of Lehigh Acres, a Jamaican national and lawful permanent resident since April 22, 2018. She registered on Sept. 30, 2024, falsely affirming citizenship, and voted in person on Nov. 5, 2024. She later signed a Citizenship Acknowledgement Form admitting she is not a U.S. citizen. She is charged with Unqualified Noncitizen Electors Willfully Voting and False Swearing.
Mei-Lin Johnston, 57, of Okeechobee, an Indonesian alien and lawful permanent resident since May 10, 2022. She registered on Nov. 8, 2022, falsely affirming she was a U.S. citizen, and voted in person on Oct. 24, 2024. She later signed a form admitting she is not a U.S. citizen. She is charged with Unqualified Noncitizen Electors Willfully Voting.
Alejandro Garcia Villegas, 65, of Lake City, a Mexican alien and lawful permanent resident since March 16, 2021. He registered on Sept. 20, 2022, falsely affirming he was a U.S. citizen and voted early in person on Oct. 30, 2024. He later agreed in writing that he is not a U.S. citizen. He is charged with Unqualified Noncitizen Electors Willfully Voting.
Manon M. Raux, 62, of Naples, a Canadian alien and lawful permanent resident since Aug. 16, 1991. She registered on Sept. 18, 2024, falsely affirming citizenship, and voted early in person on Oct. 29, 2024. She later signed a form admitting she is ineligible to register to vote. She is charged with Unqualified Noncitizen Electors Willfully Voting and False Swearing.
James Mark Hollingsworth, 62, of Lake Suzy, a Canadian alien and lawful permanent resident since Oct. 29, 1967. He registered through the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles in 2021 and voted by mail in both the 2024 primary and 2024 general elections. After receiving a non-citizen notice, he emailed the supervisor of elections acknowledging he is a permanent resident and stating he had been voting in Florida. He is charged with two counts of Unqualified Noncitizen Electors Willfully Voting and two counts of False Swearing.
Giorgio Dominique Mucelli, 66, of Melbourne, a Canadian alien and lawful permanent resident who is charged with Voting by a Non-Citizen Elector and False Affirmation after voting in seven general elections from 2004 through 2024, including the 2024 general election.
Each charged offense is a third-degree felony. If convicted, most of the defendants face up to 10 years in the Florida Department of Corrections. Hollingsworth faces up to 20 years.
The cases will be prosecuted by the Office of Statewide Prosecution, with cases spanning across Brevard, Collier, Columbia, DeSoto, Hillsborough, Lee, Marion, Okeechobee, and Polk Counties.
The investigation remains active and ongoing. Additional charges and arrests are expected.

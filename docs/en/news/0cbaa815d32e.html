DHS Proposes New Fees of Up to $70,000 for F-1 Student OPT Work Authorization

Published Oct 7, 2026

DHS has issued a proposed rule to impose new fees on F-1 nonimmigrant students participating in Optional Practical Training (OPT). The rule, which is not yet final, would set a $70,000 fee for initial OPT and a $30,000 fee for subsequent OPT.

Key points

  • The proposed fee is $70,000 per student for initial OPT and $30,000 for any subsequent OPT.
  • The fee is not tied to a specific employer but applies whenever a school recommends a student for OPT in SEVIS.
  • USCIS would not grant employment authorization unless the school has paid the required fee.
  • DHS says the fees are intended to reduce fraud and abuse, including problematic worksites and 'pay-to-stay' visa schemes.
  • Public comments are accepted from October 8 through November 9, with a separate 60-day comment period for the Paperwork Reduction Act section.
  • This is only a proposed rule and is not yet final or in effect.

Who is affected

F-1 nonimmigrant students seeking or currently in OPT, SEVP-certified schools, designated school officials, and employers are affected.

What to do

Interested parties should review the Notice of Proposed Rulemaking in the Federal Register and submit comments before the November 9 deadline.

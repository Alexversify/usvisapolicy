ICE Arrests Dozens of Noncitizens in Nationwide Voter Fraud Crackdown

Published Oct 28, 2026

DHS said ICE arrested dozens of noncitizens across the country accused of illegally registering to vote or voting in U.S. elections. The release lists 13 example cases, several involving prior criminal convictions, and cites additional arrests, indictments and a conviction from March through October 2026 in multiple states. DHS said it directed ICE in June to apply stricter penalties, including deportation, to noncitizens who vote illegally.

Key points

  • DHS said ICE arrested dozens of noncitizens nationwide on allegations of illegally registering to vote or voting in U.S. elections.
  • The release lists 13 example cases involving people from countries including Honduras, Mexico, Cuba, Vietnam and Cambodia, several with prior criminal convictions.
  • DHS also cited additional arrests, indictments and one conviction from March through October 2026 involving noncitizens accused of illegal voting in several states.
  • One case involved a naturalized U.S. citizen arrested for voting in New Jersey before she became a citizen.
  • DHS said that in June it directed ICE to apply stricter penalties, including deportation, for noncitizens who vote illegally.
  • Secretary Mullin said he sent letters in July to state election officials warning that tens of thousands of noncitizens are registered to vote.

Who is affected

Noncitizens who have registered to vote or voted in U.S. elections, including green card holders and people later naturalized, and anyone facing related ICE investigation or charges.

What to do

People who are not U.S. citizens should not register or vote in any U.S. election, and anyone contacted by ICE or facing related charges should seek legal counsel immediately.

Dozens of Illegal Aliens Arrested In Nationwide Voter Fraud Crackdown
Release Date: October 28, 2026
The federal government is investigating alleged violations of U.S. election laws by aliens who admitted to voting in American elections
WASHINGTON — The United States Department of Homeland Security (DHS) released the following statement announcing that U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) has arrested dozens of individuals in connection with alleged voter fraud and election-related offenses across the United States, including numerous cases of aliens admitting to unlawfully registering to vote or casting ballots in American elections.
“The patriots of ICE have arrested dozens of non-citizens who illegally voted in American elections,” said DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin. “Some of them have criminal histories that include assault with a dangerous weapon, domestic violence, drug trafficking, burglary, and driving under the influence. The Trump Administration will not allow illegal aliens to steal the votes of hardworking American citizens. American elections are for Americans only.”
Below are 13 examples of ICE’s ongoing investigations and arrests of illegal aliens who committed voter fraud:
Juan Carlos Matute-Hernandez, a criminal illegal alien from Honduras who illegally voted in the 2026 election. His criminal history includes an arrest for driving offenses.
Aden Hassan Aden, a criminal alien from Somalia who illegally voted in the 2020 election. His criminal history includes convictions for domestic violence, drug possession, and larceny.
Arturo Salazar Garcia, an illegal alien from Mexico who illegally voted in the 2024 election.
Jaimie Sosa-Sardina, a criminal alien from Cuba who illegally voted in the 2024 election. His criminal history includes convictions for selling cocaine, battery, and resisting an officer.
Marva Marie Cook, a criminal alien from Germany who illegally voted in the 2022 election. Her criminal history includes a conviction for larceny.
Braulio Osiel Zapata Perez, a criminal illegal alien from Nicaragua who illegally voted in the 2024 election. His criminal history includes an arrest for DUI.
Mai Tran, a criminal illegal alien from Vietnam who illegally voted in the 2024 election. Her criminal history includes convictions for fraud, numerous drug possession charges, selling cocaine, burglary, larceny, and illegal use of credit cards.
Jose Luis Matute Chaviel, a criminal illegal alien from Venezuela who illegally voted in the 2024 election. His criminal history includes charges for DUI and cocaine possession.
Phath Vorn, a criminal illegal alien from Cambodia who illegally voted in the 1992 election. His criminal history includes convictions for assault with intent to murder, assault with a dangerous weapon, and possession of a weapon.

Servando Alejandro-Torres, a criminal illegal alien from Mexico who illegally voted in the 2024 election. His criminal history includes convictions for disorderly conduct and bail jumping, and charges for DUI.

This is part of the Trump Administration’s broader focus on stopping non-citizens from voting in American elections. Recent examples include:
In October, HSI arrested five non-citizens charged with illegally voting in Florida.

In October, HSI arrested Andres Garcia, an alien from Cuba, who registered to vote and then illegally voted in 2022 and 2024 in Missouri.
In October, HSI arrested Paulo Jorge Silva, an alien from Portugal, who registered to vote and then illegally voted in 2024 in Pennsylvania.
In September, Secretary Mullin and Attorney General Todd Blanche held a press conference announcing federal indictments against ten non-citizens for illegally voting in Minnesota.
In September, HSI arrested Mukeshkumar Somabhai Chaudhari, a non-citizen from India, for knowingly making a false claim of U.S. citizenship to vote in the 2024 election in Minnesota.
In September, HSI and the FBI arrested Sanata Coulibaly, an alien from Senegal, who admitted to voting in 2020 and 2024 in New Jersey.
In September, an HSI investigation led to the conviction of Evelyn Novelles Miller, an alien from the Philippines, who illegally voted in the 2024 election in Pennsylvania.

In March, ICE arrested Mahady Sacko, a criminal illegal alien from Mauritania who had been illegally voting in the United States since 2008.

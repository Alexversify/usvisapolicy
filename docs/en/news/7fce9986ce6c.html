DHS Criticizes California Sanctuary Policies After Nearly 24,000 Criminal Immigrants Released From Jail

Published Nov 2, 2026

The Department of Homeland Security issued a statement criticizing California state and local officials for not honoring U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) detainers, saying this has led to the release of nearly 24,000 people with criminal histories who are in the country illegally since January 20, 2025. DHS says these individuals have more than 81,000 associated criminal charges or convictions, including homicide and sexual assault cases. The statement also cites specific recent criminal cases in California and other states.

Key points

  • DHS says California jurisdictions have released nearly 24,000 people with criminal records who are in the country illegally since January 20, 2025, after ICE lodged detainers that were not honored.
  • DHS states these cases involve more than 81,000 criminal charges or convictions in total, including 169 homicides, 147 sexual assaults, and thousands of other offenses.
  • The statement names individual cases, including people convicted of homicide, manslaughter, kidnapping, and gang-related offenses who were released from county jails despite ICE detainers.
  • DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin criticized California Governor Gavin Newsom and sanctuary jurisdiction policies in the statement.
  • The release also lists other recent criminal incidents in California and other states, including fatal traffic crashes, attributed to people in the country illegally.
  • This is a DHS public statement and does not announce any new rule, policy change, or enforcement action.

Who is affected

People involved in disputes over ICE detainer compliance between federal and California state and local officials, and the public following U.S. immigration enforcement issues.

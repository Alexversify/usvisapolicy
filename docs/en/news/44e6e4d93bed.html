Two Canadian Nationals in Idaho Charged With Illegal Voting

Published Oct 22, 2026

USCIS assisted an investigation by the U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of Idaho that led to charges against two Canadian nationals accused of voting in the 2022 general election despite not being U.S. citizens. Both defendants face felony charges carrying significant fines and potential prison time if convicted. The release notes the charges are allegations and the defendants are presumed innocent.

Key points

  • USCIS assisted the U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of Idaho in an investigation into alleged illegal voting.
  • Taralynn Kathleen Bosco, 48, of Rexburg, and Stanley Richard Walton, 82, of Chubbuck, are each accused of voting in the 2022 general election while not being U.S. citizens.
  • If convicted, each defendant faces a fine of up to $100,000 and up to one year in prison.
  • The charges are allegations only, and the defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty in court.
  • USCIS encourages the public to report suspected immigration benefit fraud using its Tip Form.

Who is affected

Non-U.S. citizens, particularly regarding eligibility to vote in U.S. elections.

What to do

No action is specified in the source beyond USCIS's standing invitation to report suspected immigration fraud through its Tip Form.

Release Date
10/22/2026
BOISE, Idaho — U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services assisted an investigation that resulted in charges against two Canadian aliens residing in eastern Idaho for unlawfully voting in the 2022 general election. The U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of Idaho made the announcement.
According to court records, Taralynn Kathleen Bosco, 48, of Rexburg, is alleged to have voted in the 2022 general election even though she was not a U.S. citizen.
In a separate case, Stanley Richard Walton, 82, of Chubbuck, is alleged to have voted in the 2022 general election, even though he was not a U.S. citizen.
If convicted of illegal voting by aliens, Bosco and Walton each face a fine up to $100,000, and up to one year in prison.
Charges are merely allegations. All defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty beyond a reasonable doubt in a court of law.
To report suspected immigration benefit fraud or abuse to USCIS, please use the USCIS Tip Form.
For more information on USCIS and its programs, please visit uscis.gov or follow us on X, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook and LinkedIn.

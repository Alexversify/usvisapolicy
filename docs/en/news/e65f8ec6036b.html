DOJ Files Record 40 Denaturalization Cases Against Naturalized Citizens With Hidden Crimes

Published Oct 13, 2026

The Department of Justice, working with USCIS, filed denaturalization cases against 40 naturalized U.S. citizens, the largest number filed in a single period under the current administration. The cases, filed in multiple federal district courts over the past two months, allege the individuals concealed serious crimes, including sexual abuse of minors, manslaughter, marriage fraud and visa fraud, during their naturalization process. Under the Immigration and Nationality Act, citizenship can be revoked if it was obtained illegally or through concealment of material facts or false statements.

Key points

  • DOJ and USCIS jointly filed 40 denaturalization cases in federal district courts over the past two months.
  • Alleged conduct includes acting as an agent of a foreign government, voluntary manslaughter, sexual abuse of minors, marriage fraud and fraudulent H-1B employment used to obtain immigration benefits.
  • The government alleges each person lied, gave false testimony, or concealed material facts during naturalization proceedings or earlier immigration applications.
  • Denaturalization is a civil court process under the INA, separate from criminal prosecution, that can revoke citizenship and cancel the certificate of naturalization.
  • These are filed complaints or cases, not final court rulings, and each defendant can respond in court.

Who is affected

Naturalized U.S. citizens named in these filings, and more broadly immigration attorneys and naturalized citizens following enforcement trends.

What to do

Anyone contacted regarding a denaturalization proceeding should consult a qualified immigration attorney promptly; no action is required from the general public.

U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services partnered with the Department of Justice in its filing of denaturalization actions against dozens of individuals accused of serious offenses, including acting as an agent for a foreign government, committing voluntary manslaughter, and aggravated sodomy against a special‑needs man.
Under the Immigration and Nationality Act, a naturalized U.S. citizen’s citizenship may be revoked and their certificate of naturalization canceled if the naturalization was illegally obtained or obtained by hiding important facts or by lying. The charges below were filed over the past couple of months.

Duran Mejia entered the United States in 2007 and naturalized in 2018. In 2019, she was charged with conspiracy to commit money laundering. During proceedings she admitted involvement in the illegal enterprise before she became a citizen. She was convicted in 2020 of conspiracy to commit money laundering for her efforts in concealing proceeds from unlawful activity. During naturalization proceedings, she lied about her criminal acts and provided false testimony.

On or about Feb. 26, 2020, Lumsden knowingly transported a computer file with the visual depiction of a minor engaging in sexually explicit conduct. On Aug. 11, 2020, Lumsden admitted to law enforcement that he first viewed child pornography five to 10 years before Aug. 11, 2020, and admitted to conducting searches for child pornography before naturalizing. On Dec. 2, 2020, Lumsden was charged with one count of transportation of child pornography. He pleaded guilty on Aug. 27, 2021, and was sentenced to 108 months in federal prison. During the naturalization process, Lumsden lied about his criminal history and lied when he indicated he never provided false, fraudulent or misleading information to a U.S. Government official.

ICE HSI, Nebraska State Patrol Secure 50-Year Sentence for Child Exploitation Offender
Release Date: November 20, 2026
WASHINGTON — The United States Department of Homeland Security (DHS) released the following statement after U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement's (ICE) Homeland Security Investigations (HSI), working with the Nebraska State Patrol, secured a 50-year federal prison sentence for an Omaha man convicted of producing and possessing child sexual abuse material (CSAM).
Justin A. Dueling, 42, was sentenced November 4 in federal court in Omaha to 600 months (50 years) in prison, followed by 15 years of supervised release. There is no parole in the federal system.
Justin A. Dueling. Photo courtesy of the Nebraska State Patrol
The Nebraska State Patrol led the investigation after receiving a CyberTip from an online platform regarding the upload of apparent CSAM. The lead investigator was an HSI task force officer assigned to the Nebraska State Patrol who used federal task force officer authorities to pursue the federal prosecution. HSI assisted the investigation and supported the execution of a search warrant at Dueling’s residence.
During the November 2025 search, investigators seized several electronic devices. Dueling admitted to sending and receiving large quantities of CSAM online. A forensic examination of the seized devices uncovered several thousand images and videos of CSAM, including material involving an identified child victim. Investigators later recovered approximately 1,650 additional CSAM images from electronic devices found in Dueling’s possession following his arrest.
The case was brought as part of Project Safe Childhood, a Department of Justice initiative that brings together federal, state, and local resources to investigate and prosecute child exploitation offenses and identify and rescue victims.
HSI recently helped secure significant sentences in five additional child exploitation cases across the United States, including life imprisonment and multiple decades-long federal prison sentences.
“Protecting children is of the highest priority to HSI,” said HSI Acting Executive Associate Director John A. Condon. “These sentences demonstrate our commitment to identifying victims, investigating those who exploit them and working with our law enforcement partners to hold offenders accountable.”
Recent HSI investigations resulted in the following sentences:
Florida: HSI Fort Pierce special agents secured a life sentence in a Florida state case involving the sexual abuse of a 10-year-old child and production of CSAM. HSI led the investigation, which identified the child victim and resulted in the offender being held accountable.
Texas: HSI Dallas special agents, with significant assistance from the Forney and Arlington police departments, secured a 35-year federal prison sentence for an offender convicted of producing CSAM. The investigation began after local law enforcement reported allegations of child sexual assault. A forensic review of electronic devices identified evidence that the offender coerced a minor to produce CSAM and helped investigators identify three minor victims.
New Jersey: HSI New Jersey special agents secured a 50-year federal prison sentence without the possibility of parole for an offender who pleaded guilty to producing and receiving CSAM. A border search and subsequent forensic examination of electronic devices uncovered communications and digital evidence documenting the abuse of minor children. The sentence also includes 10 years of supervised release and mandatory sex-offender registration.
Kentucky: HSI Lexington special agents secured 30-year federal prison sentences, on top of previously imposed 70-year state sentences, followed by lifetime supervised release, for two offenders convicted of producing CSAM involving a minor victim. Evidence uncovered the husband and wife had a handwritten contract that detailed what sexual acts they were allowed to perform on the child. HSI led the investigation and worked to ensure accountability in a case involving one of the most vulnerable victims.
Pennsylvania: HSI Philadelphia special agents, with assistance from the Northampton Borough Police Department, secured a 50-year federal prison sentence for an offender convicted on 24 counts of producing and possessing CSAM. The investigation began after HSI received allegations involving two children. A forensic examination of seized devices located numerous images and videos documenting the offenses.
These cases reflect HSI’s broader nationwide efforts to identify child victims, investigate offenders and pursue accountability for online child sexual exploitation and abuse. Supporting these efforts, the DHS Cyber Crimes Center coordinates specialized capabilities, technology and partnerships that advance investigations across the country.
The DHS Cyber Crimes Center leads efforts to combat online child sexual exploitation and abuse by coordinating investigations, leveraging advanced technology, and working with law enforcement partners to identify victims and bring offenders to justice. In fiscal year 2026, HSI initiated more than 7,200 child sexual exploitation and abuse investigations. Through these efforts, HSI made 4,989 arrests and identified or rescued more than 1,900 victims; these figures represent the highest totals since the creation of the Department of Homeland Security.
Know2Protect is a national public awareness campaign dedicated to preventing and combating online child sexual exploitation and abuse through education, prevention and public-private partnerships. Since launching in April 2024, the Department of Homeland Security’s Know2Protect campaign, led by HSI at the DHS Cyber Crimes Center, has:
Secured more than 30 signed partnerships with technology, professional sports, youth-serving and law enforcement organizations, as well as nonprofits and nongovernmental organizations;
Attracted 2.6 million visitors to Know2Protect.gov;
Driven nearly 46,000 clicks to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children’s CyberTipline; and
Delivered more than 3,300 Project iGuardian presentations to approximately 298,000 children, teens, parents and trusted adults domestically and internationally. These efforts resulted in 263 victim disclosures, 122 investigative leads and 262 cases initiated.
The campaign’s Pledge2Protect initiative has generated more than 415,000 pledges to help keep children safe online, with teenagers accounting for more than 90% of pledges. The campaign also launched Identify2Protect, which invites the public to review carefully sanitized, non-explicit images connected to child exploitation investigations. A familiar object, location, logo or other small detail could help investigators identify a victim and advance an investigation. To date, the initiative has attracted nearly 15,000 webpage visitors and generated 88 tips.
Together, these initiatives reinforce DHS’s commitment to preventing online child sexual exploitation and abuse, identifying victims and holding offenders accountable.
HSI is a global law enforcement agency responsible for investigating transnational crime and threats, including child exploitation. HSI works closely with federal, state, local and international partners to identify victims, investigate offenders and protect children from exploitation.
Members of the public who suspect a child is being sexually exploited should contact local law enforcement, the HSI Tip Line at 866-347-2423, or submit a report to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children’s CyberTipline at report.cybertip.org

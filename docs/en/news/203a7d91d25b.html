ICE Lodges Detainer for Peruvian National Arrested for Rape in New York City

Published Nov 5, 2026

DHS announced that ICE lodged a detainer on November 3, 2026 for a Peruvian national arrested by the New York Police Department in connection with an alleged rape. DHS said the individual had previously been apprehended by Border Patrol in 2023 and released into the country, and used the case to criticize New York's policies on cooperating with ICE, including a state law barring local police from partnering with the agency.

Key points

  • On November 3, 2026, ICE lodged a detainer for Reynaldo Hoel Mayna, a Peruvian national, following his arrest by the NYPD on charges including rape.
  • DHS said Mayna was first arrested by U.S. Border Patrol in August 2023 after entering near San Diego and was released into the country at that time.
  • DHS cited ICE data showing that from January 20, 2025 to October 8, 2026, New York did not honor 16,070 ICE detainers, which it said were linked to 52,503 criminal charges or convictions.
  • New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani signed an executive order limiting city cooperation with ICE, and Governor Kathy Hochul signed a law, effective August 25, 2026, barring local police departments from partnering with ICE.
  • The release lists several other criminal cases involving noncitizens in New York as additional examples.

Who is affected

This concerns ICE enforcement activity in New York State and the policy dispute over local cooperation with federal immigration enforcement; it does not change eligibility or fees for visa applicants.

ICE Lodges Detainer for Illegal Alien Arrested for Rape in New York City
Release Date: November 5, 2026
This illegal alien was RELEASED into the country by the Biden Administration
WASHINGTON – The United States Department of Homeland Security (DHS) released the following statement after U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) lodged a detainer asking authorities in New York City not to release an illegal alien accused of raping a 28-year-old woman.
On November 3, 2026, ICE lodged a detainer for Reynaldo Hoel Mayna, a criminal illegal alien from Peru, following his arrest by the New York City Police Department. His criminal history includes anal rape – strongarm, assault with a deadly weapon, forcible touching: touching sexual/intimate parts of another person, and sexual abuse – 3rd degree.
Reynaldo Hoel Mayna
On August 18, 2023, the U.S. Border Patrol arrested Mayna after he illegally entered the country near San Diego. He was then RELEASED into the country by the Biden Administration.
“This illegal alien’s criminal history includes anal rape – strongarm, assault with a deadly weapon, forcible touching: touching sexual/intimate parts of another person, and third-degree sexual abuse, yet sanctuary politicians in New York invited this vile criminal into their community,” said DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin. “Sanctuary politicians have handcuffed their law enforcement and are allowing criminal activity to thrive, while the American people become less safe. Underneath President Trump, we are restoring law and order in our cities, in our towns, and in our states, despite what happens with career politicians that put criminals in front of law-abiding citizens.”
The latest ICE data reveals that from January 20, 2025, to October 8, 2026, 16,070 detainers were not honored by the state of New York. There are 52,503 total criminal charges and convictions associated with these detainers, including:

Dangerous drugs: 4,324

New York City’s Mayor Zohran Mamdani signed an Executive Order turning New York City into a shield for violent criminal illegal aliens to reoffend and create more innocent victims.
In December 2025, then-Acting ICE Director Todd Lyons sent a letter to New York Attorney General Letitia James, along with the attorneys general of California and Illinois, putting her on notice for the state’s failure to honor ICE detainers. In response, New York Governor Kathy Hochul punished local law enforcement who did cooperate with ICE by proposing legislation that would bar local police departments from partnering and cooperating with ICE law enforcement, which she signed into law in May 2026. The law took effect on August 25, 2026.
Under Governor Hochul, New York has become a hotbed of illegal alien crime. Some of the recent illegal alien crimes in the sanctuary state include:
In October, ICE arrested Rudi and Mirabela Lacatusu, an illegal alien couple from Romania, after they were charged with grand larceny for carrying out more than a dozen jewelry thefts across four states. Nassau County Police turned them over to ICE custody.
In October, two illegal aliens from the Dominican Republic, Dangelo Rafael Jean Caraballo and Sneider Mejia Bautista, repeatedly stabbed an off-duty NYPD detective in an attempted robbery. Both suspects had previously been arrested in New York for crimes including reckless endangerment, reckless driving, possession of burglar’s tools, grand larceny, and criminal possession of a controlled substance, among other crimes, but both had been released before ICE could lodge detainers.
In June, ICE lodged a detainer for Aureliano Antonio Melendez Reyes, an illegal alien from El Salvador, after he was arrested for raping a 16-year-old girl in Huntington, New York.
In April, the New York City Department of Corrections refused to honor an ICE detainer for Roman Ceron Amatitla, an illegal alien from Mexico, who was charged with murder and arson in Queens County, New York.
In March, ICE arrested Bryan David Tasiguano Leon, an illegal alien from Ecuador and Latin Kings gang member, who had been released by New York sanctuary politicians despite being charged with assault.
In March, ICE lodged a detainer for Bairon Posada-Hernandez, a four-time deported illegal alien from Honduras, after he was arrested for shoving two bystanders, including an 83-year-old Air Force veteran, onto subway tracks in New York City. The Air Force veteran, Richard Williams, later died of his injuries.
In January, ICE arrested Rafael Andres Rubio Bohorquez, an illegal alien from Venezuela, who was working as a New York City Council employee despite having a criminal history that includes an arrest for assault.
In January, ICE arrested Gerardo Miguel-Mora, an illegal alien from Mexico, after he had been released by New York sanctuary politicians despite having a criminal history that includes arrests for strangulation, rape, sexual assault, burglary, grand larceny, and drug possession.

DHS Finalizes Rule Ending Automatic EAD Extensions for Renewal Applicants

Published Oct 13, 2026Effective Oct 14, 2026

DHS has issued a final rule that reaffirms, without changes, an October 30, 2025 interim final rule ending automatic extensions of Employment Authorization Documents (EADs, Form I-766) for timely filed renewals in certain categories. The document is filed for public inspection and scheduled to be published in the Federal Register on October 14, 2026, which the rule text also sets as its effective date.

Key points

  • The final rule reaffirms, without any changes, the October 30, 2025 interim final rule (IFR) that ended automatic extension of EAD validity for timely filed renewals in certain categories.
  • It applies to Form I-765 renewal applications filed on or after October 30, 2025; EADs already automatically extended before that date are not affected.
  • Automatic extensions provided by law or by Federal Register notice, such as those tied to Temporary Protected Status (TPS) employment documentation, are not changed by this rule.
  • DHS says the change is intended to ensure vetting and screening are completed before a new period of employment authorization or a new EAD is granted.
  • The rule amends 8 CFR 274a.13, revising the heading of paragraph (d) and adding a new paragraph (e).
  • The document is scheduled to be published in the Federal Register on October 14, 2026, and the rule states it is effective on that publication date.

Who is affected

People who hold an EAD in a category covered by this rule and file a timely Form I-765 renewal, and employers who rely on EAD validity for Form I-9 purposes.

What to do

Renewal applicants in affected categories should not assume work authorization continues automatically just because a renewal was filed, and should check the final rule text and USCIS guidance for how it applies to their specific category.

Executive Summary A. Basis and Purpose DHS’s mission is to safeguard the American people, our homeland, and our values with honor and integrity. In service of that mission, DHS protects the United States from threats by terrorists, criminals, smugglers, transnational criminal organizations, failed-state actors, and unpredictable lone offenders that constitute present and future threats to public safety and national security.
USCIS issues EADs to certain classes of aliens. These documents are valid for a specified period. Aliens who intend to continue their employment beyond the date specified on their EAD must generally file an application to renew their employment authorization and/or EAD.
The automatic extension of the validity of an EAD grants the alien the benefit of extending his or her expired employment authorization and/or EAD merely by filing a timely renewal EAD application and without first completing adjudicative review and related vetting, including resolution of any derogatory information identified during the vetting process. That is, the automatic extension allows for the granting of the benefit without USCIS first performing the following actions: an eligibility determination, completing vetting and screening checks, resolving potential hits of derogatory information in connection with the alien, and a determination that the employment authorization should be granted in the exercise of discretion, if applicable. On October 30, 2025, DHS issued an IFR titled, “Removal of the Automatic Extension of Employment Authorization Documents,” with a request for comment. See 90 FR 48799 (Oct. 30, 2025). The IFR amended DHS regulations to end the practice of automatically extending the validity of EADs for aliens in certain employment authorization categories who timely filed an application to renew their EAD. After careful consideration of the public comments submitted in connection with the IFR, DHS is reaffirming and finalizing the IFR without changes. The rationale and reasoning provided in the preamble for the IFR remains unchanged with respect to the regulatory amendments, and DHS, therefore, adopts such reasoning also to support this final rule. In reaffirming and finalizing the changes made in the IFR, the purpose of this rule remains the same as articulated in the IFR: to prioritize the proper vetting and screening of aliens and ensure continued eligibility before granting a new period of employment authorization and/or a new EAD by ending the practice of automatically extending the validity of employment authorization and/or EADs for certain employment authorization categories.
Ending the practice of providing automatic extensions of EADs is consistent with Section 16 of President Trump’s directive in Executive Order (E.O.) 14159, Protecting the American People Against Invasion, which directs the Secretary of Homeland Security, in coordination with the Secretary of State and the Attorney General, to take all appropriate action to align any departmental activities with the policies set out by the President and to ensure, among others, “that employment authorization is provided in a manner consistent with section 274A of the Immigration and Nationality Act (8 U.S.C. 1324a), and that employment authorization is not provided to any unauthorized alien in the United States.”1 It is also consistent with Section 2 of E.O. 14161, Protecting the United States From Foreign Terrorists and Other National Security and Public Safety Threats, which directs the Secretary of State, in coordination with the Secretary of Homeland Security, the Attorney General, and the Director of National Intelligence to “identify all resources that may be used to ensure that all aliens seeking admission to the United States, or who are already in the United States, are vetted and screened to the maximum degree possible.”2 B. This final rule reaffirms and finalizes changes made by the IFR to DHS regulations to end the practice of automatically extending the validity of employment authorization documents (Forms I-766 or EADs) for aliens who have timely filed an application to renew their EAD in certain employment authorization categories. Response: DHS declines to adopt the commenters’ suggestions. Keeping a reduced automatic extension period would conflict with DHS’s overriding concern that granting employment authorization benefits to aliens without those aliens being properly vetted and determined to continue to be eligible (and merit a favorable exercise of discretion, if applicable) based on a complete adjudicative review is contrary to the DHS mission to enhance benefit integrity and protect public safety and national security.111 As explained in the IFR,112 if DHS pursued these options, aliens with derogatory information flagged during the background check process would nevertheless still obtain an automatic extension of some lengths (such as 180 or 240 days). These extensions pose a security vulnerability and undermine benefit integrity. These same aliens could use their automatically extended employment authorization and/or EADs to obtain valid identity documents, which would make it easier to commit conduct detrimental to the United States.

Consecutive EAD Validity Periods Comment: Numerous commenters recommended implementing consecutive EAD validity periods so that applicants could file early without losing work authorization, time, and money.

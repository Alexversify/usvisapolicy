DHS Disputes NYC Mayor's Account of ICE Shooting, Cites Nearly 15,000 Unhonored Detainers

Published Oct 9, 2026

DHS issued a statement responding to New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani's comments on an October 8 incident in which an ICE officer discharged a weapon during an attempted arrest in the Bronx. DHS disputed several of the mayor's claims and said New York has failed to honor nearly 15,000 ICE detainers since January 2025.

Key points

  • ICE officers attempted to arrest Oscar Bengal, a Dominican national with a prior criminal record, in the Bronx on October 8; DHS says he drove his vehicle at officers, who then fired their weapons.
  • Bengal was treated for a neck injury, taken into custody, and placed in a detention facility outside New York State; the incident remains under investigation by FBI and HSI.
  • DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin and ICE officials rejected Mayor Mamdani's description of the incident and of ICE operations in New York City as false.
  • DHS states that from January 20, 2025 to September 14, 2026, New York did not honor 14,916 ICE detainers, which it links to 47,750 associated criminal charges or convictions.
  • DHS referenced a New York law restricting local police cooperation with ICE, signed by Governor Kathy Hochul and effective August 25, 2026, as well as several individual criminal cases it attributes to sanctuary policies.

Who is affected

The statement concerns New York State and City officials, ICE operations, local law enforcement cooperation policies, and noncitizens with criminal records in New York.

What to do

This is a DHS press statement, not a new rule; no action is required from visa applicants or employers.

Release Date: October 9, 2026
WASHINGTON – The United States Department of Homeland Security (DHS) released the following statement debunking the lies spread by New York sanctuary politicians after a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officer discharged his weapon during an attempted arrest of an illegal alien gang member in New York City. DHS also highlighted the fact that a collective total of nearly 15,000 immigration detainers issued by ICE were not honored due to sanctuary policies.
On October 8, at approximately 4:00 PM ET, ICE officers were conducting a targeted enforcement operation in the Bronx, New York, to arrest Oscar Bengal, a criminal illegal alien from the Dominican Republic. His criminal history includes a prior criminal conviction for fourth-degree grand larceny: property taken from person, and prior arrests for first-degree robbery: forcible theft armed with a deadly weapon, criminal sale of a controlled substance: narcotic drug, possession of stolen property, and assault. After his conviction, he was sentenced in April 2025 to five years of probation. Bengal has associations with the Trinitarios gang, the same gang that brutally shot a CBP officer last year. He was the target of an investigation by ICE Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) into the shooting of the CBP officer. The gang is known for theft using weapons and mopeds.
ICE officers attempted to arrest Bengal and used their vehicles to block him in. He refused lawful orders to exit his vehicle, then accelerated and smashed into two government vehicles. He then weaponized his vehicle and accelerated toward other officers. The officers, believing that their lives and the lives of their partners and the general public were in danger, used their training and fired their weapons. Bengal was superficially struck by one of the bullets or fragments in the neck. He stopped his vehicle, at which point ICE officers arrested him, rendered first aid, and then took him to the hospital. Because of his lengthy criminal history and history of fleeing law enforcement, ICE placed him in a detention facility outside the state of New York.
The Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) and Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) arrived on the scene shortly thereafter. The incident remains under investigation.

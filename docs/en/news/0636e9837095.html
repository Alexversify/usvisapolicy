DHS Statement Criticizes New York Sanctuary Policies, Cites Crime Cases

Published Oct 12, 2026

DHS issued a press statement criticizing Governor Kathy Hochul's sanctuary policies in New York, citing several criminal cases involving noncitizens without legal status who had prior contact with local police or ICE detainers. The release does not announce any new rule, fee, or change to immigration procedures.

Key points

  • DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin stated that New York's sanctuary policies have allowed illegal aliens to remain free despite ICE detainers.
  • The release lists several cases, including the killing of Laken Riley in Georgia and the subway platform death of Richard Williams in New York City, to illustrate the agency's argument.
  • DHS said some individuals named had prior arrests by the NYPD and were released before ICE could lodge detainers.
  • The statement is a political and policy criticism, not a new regulation, proclamation, or operational change.
  • No new fees, deadlines, or eligibility changes are announced in this release.

Who is affected

The statement concerns New York State sanctuary jurisdiction policy and ICE detainer practices, and does not directly change requirements for visa applicants or employers.

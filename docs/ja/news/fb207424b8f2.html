DHS、難民資格取消通知への回答期限を30日から10日に短縮

公開日 2026年10月13日施行日 2026年10月14日

米国土安全保障省(DHS)は8 CFR Part 207を改正する暫定最終規則を発表し、難民資格の取消意向通知(NOIT)に対する回答期限を30日から10日へ短縮しました。本規則は連邦官報に掲載された日から発効する一方、意見公募も行われます。

ポイント

  • 難民資格の取消意向通知(NOIT)への回答期限が30日から10日に短縮されます。
  • 本規則は暫定最終規則であり、連邦官報掲載日に発効しますが、まだ最終確定したものではありません。
  • 意見提出期限は掲載日から30日以内とされていますが、原文には具体的な日付は記載されていません。
  • 意見はregulations.govを通じて英語で提出し、DHS Docket No. USCIS-2026-0397を明記する必要があります。
  • DHSは前政権下で難民審査手続きが緩められ、不正リスクが高まったとされる調査事例を改正の根拠として挙げています。
  • 難民資格そのものの認定基準は変更されず、取消手続きにおける回答期限のみが短縮されます。

対象者

既に難民として米国に入国しており、今後取消意向通知を受け取る可能性がある人、およびその代理弁護士。

取るべき対応

対象者とその弁護士は、取消意向通知を受け取った場合に新たな10日以内の期限で対応できるよう準備し、関係者は掲載日から30日以内にregulations.govを通じて意見を提出することができます。

II. Executive Summary A. Purpose of the Regulatory Action The purpose of this rulemaking is to shorten the termination notice period in order to mitigate potential risks posed by aliens admitted to the United States as refugees when, in fact, they were not eligible for refugee classification at the time of admission. Those who are improperly admitted may not have their identity fully confirmed and vetted due to an incomplete record, and as such pose a risk to program integrity and may pose a risk to national security and public safety. A review of refugee processing policies and procedures implemented under the Biden administration indicates an alarming erosion of integrity leading to aliens being admitted to the United States as refugees when their identities, eligibility for refugee classification, and admissibility were determined based on incomplete records.1 Operational guidance implemented during this period allowed officers to forgo typical lines of inquiry, sacrificing the caliber and efficacy of the refugee interview, a critical screening component of the refugee adjudication process.2 The priority of expediency over quality encouraged a lack of robust interviews. B. 難民資格の終了 米国に難民として条件付きで入国を許可された外国人の資格をUSCISが終了させる唯一の根拠は、USCISが、当該外国人が米国への入国時に難民の定義を満たしていなかったと判断した場合である。15 法律上、他の終了事由は規定されていない。入国時に実際には難民の定義を満たしていなかった外国人は入国不許可であったことになり、その資格が終了すれば、通常、米国に滞在するための法的根拠を欠くことになる。16 外国人が米国への入国時に難民の定義を満たしていなかったことを理由とする難民資格終了の判定は、主たる難民についてのみ適用され、随伴難民には適用されない。難民資格を有するためには、主たる申請者は、INA101条(a)(42)、8 U.S.C. 1101(a)(42)に定める難民の定義の要件を満たし、米国への入国が許可される者であり、かつ、指定された米国難民受入プログラム(USRAP)処理優先順位のいずれかに該当する者でなければならない。17 USCISは、主たる難民の資格が終了した場合、随伴難民の資格を併せて終了させることができる。18 難民資格は、当該外国人が難民資格のままであり、いまだ合法永住者(LPR)資格への変更または帰化をしていない間に限り終了させることができる。19 ただし、USCISがINA246条、8 U.S.C. 1156に基づき外国人のLPR資格を取り消した場合、難民として入国を許可された外国人は、以前の難民資格に戻り、終了の根拠が法定要件を満たす場合にはその難民資格を終了させることができる。20 15 INA207条(c)(4)、8 U.S.C. 1157(c)(4)。
B. Termination of Refugee Status The sole basis for USCIS to terminate the status of an alien conditionally admitted to the United States as a refugee is if USCIS determines that the alien did not meet the definition of a refugee at the time of admission to the United States.15 No other ground of termination is specified under the law. An alien who did not, in fact, meet the refugee definition at the time of admission would have been inadmissible, and once that status is terminated, will generally lack legal grounds to remain in the United States.16 The determination to terminate refugee status because the alien did not meet the definition of a refugee at the time of his or her admission to the United States applies solely to principal refugees and not to derivative refugees. In order to be eligible for refugee status, principal applicants must meet the requirements of the refugee definition in INA 101(a)(42), 8 U.S.C. 1101(a)(42), must be admissible to the United States, and must qualify under one of the designated USRAP processing priorities.17 USCIS may in turn terminate a derivative refugee’s status if the principal’s status is terminated.18 Refugee status can only be terminated while the alien remains in refugee status and has not yet adjusted to lawful permanent resident (LPR) status or naturalized.19 However, if USCIS has rescinded an alien’s LPR status under INA 246, 8 U.S.C. 1156, an alien admitted as a refugee reverts to the prior refugee status and his or her refugee status can be terminated if the basis for termination meets the statutory requirements.20 15 INA 207(c)(4), 8 U.S.C. 1157(c)(4).

In 2017, President Trump issued Executive Order 13769, Protecting the Nation from Foreign Terrorist Entry into the United States, restricting entry to the U.S. by certain foreign nationals and temporarily suspending the USRAP.35 During this suspension of refugee processing agencies were directed to review and strengthen vetting procedures. As a result of the review, USCIS clarified and confirmed that as a matter of policy, officers interviewing refugee applicants were required to elicit sufficient information to make a determination on both the past persecution and well-founded fear of future persecution claims. Gathering testimony on both past persecution and well- founded fear ensures a thorough and accurate evaluation of the applicant’s eligibility and prevents overlooking critical facts that could substantiate, or call into question, their claim. For example, thorough elicitation of testimony regarding past and future harm may help identify inconsistencies and potential fraud by allowing adjudicators to probe the alien’s narrative for credibility by ensuring it is internally consistent and consistent with statements the alien may have provided to processing partners prior to interview. Most importantly, it requires officers to probe more thoroughly into an applicant’s background and experiences, helping to establish his or her identity, and further ensure that the correct decision is made in his or her case. Although eliciting testimony about both past persecution and well-founded fear helps develop a fuller picture of the alien and better assess eligibility—ensuring that protection is granted to those who genuinely 35 Protecting the Nation From Foreign Terrorist Entry Into the United States, 82 FR 8977 (Feb. 1, 2017). qualify under the law—during the Biden administration this policy was rescinded. Refugee officers were no longer required to elicit testimony on both past persecution and well-founded fear claims, and were instead instructed to only pursue one or the other, once one had been established, precluding opportunities to identify critical information and concerns related to an alien’s eligibility.

In addition to the relaxed requirements for principal refugee applicants, USCIS changed policies regarding derivative applicants. The first Trump administration issued guidance emphasizing the importance of eliciting testimony from derivative I-590 applicants apart from the principal refugee applicant to further explore potential national security, identity, inadmissibility, and credibility issues. However, USCIS subsequently rescinded this guidance under the Biden administration and provided refugee officers the sole discretion on when to interview derivative applicants separately. The purpose of this change was to reduce the amount of questions and time spent with derivatives in an interview, thus not thoroughly vetting and identifying potential issues of all members of a case who, upon case approval, were eligible to travel to the United States as refugees along with the principal applicant.

During the first Trump administration, USCIS reduced the number of refugee interviews conducted per day per officer for certain cases to allow for further exploration of potential national security, inadmissibility, and credibility issues at interviews for refugee adjudications. Notwithstanding, USCIS under the Biden administration, in their effort to streamline interviews and increase the volume of refugee cases that could be processed, increased the number of cases an officer was expected to interview and adjudicate in a day for certain cases, again pressuring officers to at times scale back interviews to comply with the expectations.

Refugee officers potentially missed vital information that could impact an applicant’s eligibility, including issues related to fraud, public safety, and national security, and discretionary factors. USCIS, under the Biden administration, failed to fulfill its responsibility to ensure that its officers thoroughly assessed applicants, potentially resulting in an influx of ineligible aliens into the country that may pose a risk to the national interests of the United States.

IV. Discussion of Interim Final Rule Previous administrations’ prioritization of the admissions of large numbers of refugees over homeland security blatantly overlooked essential components of eligibility and put at risk the national security, public safety and other interests of the U.S. citizenry. Safeguarding national security and public safety remains the foremost priority of the country’s immigration programs, taking precedence over numerical admission targets. The agency stands committed to conducting rigorous vetting of all aliens and to the detection and prevention of fraud and other serious threats, ensuring the safety of all Americans prior to admission of an alien to the United States.
したがって、本暫定最終規則(IFR)は、上記第III.C節で述べた不十分な審査によって生じたリスクに鑑み、国家安全保障及び公共の安全を保護するために難民資格の取消しができる限り迅速に行われることを確保すべく、難民がNOIT(取消意思通知)に対応するために与えられる期間を短縮するものである。本IFRの下では、難民は引き続きNOITを受領するものとし、同通知には、当該難民が入国(受入れ)時点において難民の定義を満たしていなかったとの判断に基づき、DHSがその難民資格を取り消す意思を有する旨が明記される。DHSは、USCISが当該難民が入国時点において難民の定義を満たしていなかったと認定した具体的な理由(例えば、過去に受けた危害が迫害に該当するほど深刻であったことを立証できなかったこと、当該外国人の受けた危害が保護事由に基づくものではないと判断されたこと、難民の定義の要素の一部又は全部について信用性のある証言を提供できなかったこと等)を示すものとする。もっとも、本規則は、当該外国人が希望する場合に、これに対応する(書面による証拠を提出し、又は口頭による証拠の提出を希望する旨を示す)ための期間として、30日ではなく10日を与えるものとする。NOITへの回答期限が応答のないまま経過した場合、当該難民はNOITにおける主張に反論する権利を放棄したものとみなされ、DHSはその難民資格を取り消すものとする。
Accordingly, this IFR shortens the amount of time that refugees are provided to respond to a NOIT in order to ensure that terminations of refugee status can occur as expeditiously as possible to protect national security and public safety, given the risks created by the deficient vetting described above in Section III.C. Under this IFR, refugees will continue to receive a NOIT, which will specify DHS’s intent to terminate their refugee status based on a determination that they did not meet the refugee definition at the time of admission. DHS will provide the specific reason or reasons why USCIS found the refugee failed to meet the refugee definition at the time of admission (e.g., failure to establish that past harm experienced was serious enough to rise to the level of persecution, a determination that the alien’s harm was not on account of a protected ground, failure to provide credible testimony as to any or all of the elements of the refugee definition, etc.). However, this rule will provide such aliens with ten days (rather than 30 days) to respond (provide documentary evidence or indicate a desire to provide oral evidence) if they choose. If the deadline to reply to the NOIT passes without a response, the refugee is considered to have forfeited his or her right to rebut the allegations in the NOIT, and DHS will terminate his or her refugee status.

As USCIS continues to encounter aliens admitted as refugees who did not meet the definition of refugee at the time of admission, termination of refugee status in as prompt and efficient manner as possible ensures USCIS can best protect the safety and security of Americans and continue complying with E.O.s 14161 and 14163. By shortening the 30-day period for an alien to respond to a NOIT to 10 days, USCIS is exercising its authority to expeditiously terminate the status of refugees who did not meet the refugee definition at the time of their admission to the United States. DHS believes that reducing the NOIT response period from 30 days to 10 days maintains adequate procedural safeguards for aliens by affording them an opportunity to respond to the NOIT while also balancing the government’s interests in national security and public safety and in protecting the integrity of the refugee program. The NOIT will inform the alien the basis for termination and explain that they may submit evidence to contest termination, and/or request an interview. An alien does not need to submit evidence or a full response within the 10 days, and may instead opt for an interview, but must make the interview request to USCIS within the response period. Aliens who receive a NOIT under the proposed process therefore will continue to receive a meaningful opportunity to respond to the allegations in the notice—the alien can submit a written response to the NOIT within the deadline and/or request an interview with USCIS where their refugee claim can be heard. The abbreviated time period to respond to the NOIT will in turn allow the government to more efficiently process cases where aliens did not meet the refugee definition at the time of admission to the United States, and who were from the outset, never eligible for the benefit they received. Instead of waiting 30 days for an alien to respond to a NOIT before scheduling an interview if requested by the alien or taking other action, this rule will allow USCIS to act as soon as 10 days after service of the NOIT. For aliens who do not respond timely, the 10-day response period will allow USCIS to finalize termination much more quickly. This IFR would accordingly more quickly eliminate a pathway to permanent status used by aliens with falsified claims or malevolent intent, including those who may pose an ongoing and imminent threat to public safety and national security such as the refugee cases reviewed and terminated by the USCIS Vetting Center outlined above.
